_Curated 2026-11. ⭐ = essential (read first). Links: DOI for journal papers, arXiv abs page otherwise._

_Scope: inorganic crystals, catalysts, MOFs, and the discovery stack built on them. Interatomic potentials (MACE, CHGNet, M3GNet, UMA), their datasets (MPtrj, OMat24) and Matbench Discovery are covered in the MLIP list (`mlip.md`)._

## 1. Foundations: High-Throughput DFT, Databases & Field Overviews
1. [The high-throughput highway to computational materials design](https://doi.org/10.1038/nmat3568) — Curtarolo et al., *Nature Materials* 2013. The case for the high-throughput paradigm (generate, screen, verify) that every later ML-for-materials effort sits on.
2. ⭐ [Commentary: The Materials Project: A materials genome approach to accelerating materials innovation](https://doi.org/10.1063/1.4812323) — Jain et al., *APL Materials* 2013. The database almost every model in this list trains on; explains how its DFT data is calculated and what it can and cannot be trusted for.
3. [Materials Design and Discovery with High-Throughput Density Functional Theory: The Open Quantum Materials Database (OQMD)](https://doi.org/10.1007/s11837-013-0755-4) — Saal et al., *JOM* 2013. OQMD and how formation energies are checked against experiment, which is the origin of the "convex hull" stability language.
4. [AFLOW: An automatic framework for high-throughput materials discovery](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.commatsci.2012.02.005) — Curtarolo et al., *Computational Materials Science* 2012. How an automated DFT workflow framework is built, including prototypes and standardized settings.
5. [The joint automated repository for various integrated simulations (JARVIS) for data-driven materials design](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41524-020-00440-1) — Choudhary et al., *npj Computational Materials* 2020. NIST's combined DFT, force-field and ML repository; a source of many benchmark properties (optics, elastic, 2D).
6. [Machine-Learning-Assisted Determination of the Global Zero-Temperature Phase Diagram of Materials](https://doi.org/10.1002/adma.202210788) — Schmidt et al., *Advanced Materials* 2023. The Alexandria database: ML-guided DFT screening of about 1 billion candidates that greatly expanded the known convex hull.
7. [The thermodynamic scale of inorganic crystalline metastability](https://doi.org/10.1126/sciadv.1600225) — Sun et al., *Science Advances* 2016. Why "on the hull" is not the same as "synthesizable"; how far above the hull real materials actually sit.
8. ⭐ [Machine learning for molecular and materials science](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-018-0337-2) — Butler et al., *Nature* 2018. The standard entry-point review: the data, representation and model concepts for the whole field.

## 2. Crystal Representations & Property Prediction
9. [Fast and Accurate Modeling of Molecular Atomization Energies with Machine Learning](https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevLett.108.058301) — Rupp et al., *Physical Review Letters* 2012. The Coulomb matrix and kernel regression; the origin of "learn DFT energies from structure".
10. [On representing chemical environments](https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevB.87.184115) — Bartók, Kondor & Csányi, *Physical Review B* 2013. SOAP and the invariance requirements that every descriptor and GNN must meet.
11. [A general-purpose machine learning framework for predicting properties of inorganic materials](https://doi.org/10.1038/npjcompumats.2016.28) — Ward et al., *npj Computational Materials* 2016. Magpie composition features plus random forests, still a strong baseline you must beat.
12. ⭐ [Crystal Graph Convolutional Neural Networks for an Accurate and Interpretable Prediction of Material Properties](https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevLett.120.145301) — Xie & Grossman, *Physical Review Letters* 2018. CGCNN, the model that made periodic crystal graphs the default representation.
13. [SchNet – A deep learning architecture for molecules and materials](https://doi.org/10.1063/1.5019779) — Schütt et al., *Journal of Chemical Physics* 2018. Continuous-filter convolutions; shows a message-passing network carrying over from molecules to crystal property prediction.
14. [Graph Networks as a Universal Machine Learning Framework for Molecules and Crystals](https://doi.org/10.1021/acs.chemmater.9b01294) — Chen et al., *Chemistry of Materials* 2019. MEGNet: global-state graph networks, transfer learning, and learned elemental embeddings.
15. [Atomistic Line Graph Neural Network for improved materials property predictions](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41524-021-00650-1) — Choudhary & DeCost, *npj Computational Materials* 2021. ALIGNN: adding bond angles through line graphs; long a top performer on JARVIS and Matbench.
16. [Predicting materials properties without crystal structure: deep representation learning from stoichiometry](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-020-19964-7) — Goodall & Lee, *Nature Communications* 2020. Roost: composition-only message passing, essential when the structure is unknown (the actual discovery setting).
17. [Compositionally restricted attention-based network for materials property predictions](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41524-021-00545-1) — Wang et al., *npj Computational Materials* 2021. CrabNet: a transformer over element tokens; attention for composition-based prediction.
18. ⭐ [Benchmarking materials property prediction methods: the Matbench test set and Automatminer reference algorithm](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41524-020-00406-3) — Dunn et al., *npj Computational Materials* 2020. Matbench, the standard benchmark for property prediction; how to evaluate fairly across 13 tasks.
19. ⭐ [A critical examination of compound stability predictions from machine-learned formation energies](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41524-020-00362-y) — Bartel et al., *npj Computational Materials* 2020. Shows that a low formation-energy MAE does not give correct stability (hull) predictions. Required reading before claiming discovery.
20. [Can machine learning identify the next high-temperature superconductor? Examining extrapolation performance for materials discovery](https://doi.org/10.1039/C8ME00012C) — Meredig et al., *Molecular Systems Design & Engineering* 2018. Leave-cluster-out cross-validation: random splits overstate how well models extrapolate to new chemistries.

## 3. Discovery at Scale & the Novelty Debate
21. ⭐ [Scaling deep learning for materials discovery](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-023-06735-9) — Merchant et al., *Nature* 2023. GNoME: active-learning GNNs plus DFT yielding about 380k "stable" crystals; the landmark of scale.
22. [Accelerating Computational Materials Discovery with Machine Learning and Cloud High-Performance Computing: from Large-Scale Screening to Experimental Validation](https://doi.org/10.1021/jacs.4c03849) — Chen et al., *Journal of the American Chemical Society* 2024. Microsoft/PNNL funnel from 32M candidates to a synthesized Na/Li solid electrolyte; an end-to-end screening template.
23. ⭐ [Artificial Intelligence Driving Materials Discovery? Perspective on the Article: Scaling Deep Learning for Materials Discovery](https://doi.org/10.1021/acs.chemmater.4c00643) — Cheetham & Seshadri, *Chemistry of Materials* 2024. Solid-state chemists audit a sample of GNoME's outputs; introduces the test of "novelty, credibility, utility".

## 4. Generative Crystal Models & Inverse Design
24. [Inverse design in search of materials with target functionalities](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41570-018-0121) — Zunger, *Nature Reviews Chemistry* 2018. The conceptual framing of inverse design ("property to structure") from before deep generative models.
25. [Inverse Design of Solid-State Materials via a Continuous Representation](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.matt.2019.08.017) — Noh et al., *Matter* 2019. iMatGen: an early VAE over voxelized crystals (V–O system); shows why periodicity and invariance are hard.
26. ⭐ [Crystal Diffusion Variational Autoencoder for Periodic Material Generation](https://arxiv.org/abs/2110.06197) — Xie et al., *ICLR* 2022. CDVAE: equivariant score-based decoding for periodic structures; set the standard tasks and metrics (validity, coverage) for crystal generation.
27. [Crystal Structure Prediction by Joint Equivariant Diffusion](https://arxiv.org/abs/2309.04475) — Jiao et al., *NeurIPS* 2023. DiffCSP: joint diffusion on lattice and fractional coordinates (wrapped normal), the backbone of most later models.
28. [FlowMM: Generating Materials with Riemannian Flow Matching](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.04713) — Miller et al., *ICML* 2024. Flow matching on the torus and lattice manifold; matches diffusion models with far fewer integration steps.
29. [Fine-Tuned Language Models Generate Stable Inorganic Materials as Text](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.04379) — Gruver et al., *ICLR* 2024. A fine-tuned LLaMA-2 that writes crystals as plain text is competitive with diffusion models; the LLM route to generation.
30. [Crystal structure generation with autoregressive large language modeling](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-024-54639-7) — Antunes et al., *Nature Communications* 2024. CrystaLLM: a GPT trained on millions of CIF files that generates plausible structures for unseen compositions.
31. ⭐ [A generative model for inorganic materials design](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-025-08628-5) — Zeni et al., *Nature* 2025. MatterGen: a diffusion foundation model with adapter fine-tuning for property, symmetry and chemistry conditioning, plus one experimental synthesis.
32. [Exploration of crystal chemical space using text-guided generative artificial intelligence](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-025-59636-y) — Park, Onwuli & Walsh, *Nature Communications* 2025. Chemeleon: Crystal-CLIP text–structure alignment plus classifier-free guided diffusion, applied to Li–P–S–Cl.
33. [All-atom Diffusion Transformers: Unified generative modelling of molecules and materials](https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.03965) — Joshi et al., *ICML* 2025. ADiT: one latent diffusion transformer for both crystals and molecules; the trend away from hand-built equivariance toward scale.
34. ⭐ [Establishing baselines for generative discovery of inorganic crystals](https://doi.org/10.1039/D5MH00010F) — Szymanski & Bartel, *Materials Horizons* 2025. Simple ion exchange and random enumeration often beat generative models at finding stable novel materials. Read it before publishing a generator.
35. [Continued challenges in high-throughput materials predictions: MatterGen predicts compounds from the training dataset](https://doi.org/10.1039/D6MH00268D) — Juelsholt, *Materials Horizons* 2026. Argues that MatterGen's synthesized "new" TaCr2O6 is a known disordered phase that was in its training data; a lesson on disorder and novelty checks.

## 5. Catalysts & Porous Materials (MOFs)
36. [Towards the computational design of solid catalysts](https://doi.org/10.1038/nchem.121) — Nørskov et al., *Nature Chemistry* 2009. Descriptors, scaling relations and volcano plots: the physics that catalysis ML targets.
37. [Accelerated discovery of CO2 electrocatalysts using active machine learning](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-020-2242-8) — Zhong et al., *Nature* 2020. ML-plus-DFT active learning over intermetallic surfaces leading to an experimentally record-setting Cu–Al CO2RR catalyst.
38. ⭐ [Open Catalyst 2020 (OC20) Dataset and Community Challenges](https://doi.org/10.1021/acscatal.0c04525) — Chanussot et al., *ACS Catalysis* 2021. OC20 (1.3M relaxations) and its tasks (S2EF, IS2RE); the ImageNet moment for catalysis ML.
39. [The Open Catalyst 2022 (OC22) Dataset and Challenges for Oxide Electrocatalysts](https://doi.org/10.1021/acscatal.2c05426) — Tran et al., *ACS Catalysis* 2023. Extends the effort to oxide OER catalysts, where magnetism and charge make learning much harder.
40. [AdsorbML: a leap in efficiency for adsorption energy calculations using generalizable machine learning potentials](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41524-023-01121-5) — Lan et al., *npj Computational Materials* 2023. Using ML to search adsorbate placements, with DFT to verify, about 2000× faster; the practical OC20 workflow.
41. [Open Catalyst Experiments 2024 (OCx24): Bridging Experiments and Computational Models](https://arxiv.org/abs/2411.11783) — Abed et al., *arXiv* 2024. Hundreds of synthesized and tested catalysts compared against computed descriptors; a sobering look at the computation–experiment gap.
42. [Big-Data Science in Porous Materials: Materials Genomics and Machine Learning](https://doi.org/10.1021/acs.chemrev.0c00004) — Jablonka et al., *Chemical Reviews* 2020. The definitive review of MOF/zeolite databases, descriptors and ML pitfalls.
43. [Data-driven design of metal–organic frameworks for wet flue gas CO2 capture](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-019-1798-7) — Boyd et al., *Nature* 2019. Screening of 300k+ hypothetical MOFs leading to binding-site motifs and two synthesized MOFs that work in wet flue gas.
44. [A multi-modal pre-training transformer for universal transfer learning in metal–organic frameworks](https://doi.org/10.1038/s42256-023-00628-2) — Kang et al., *Nature Machine Intelligence* 2023. MOFTransformer: pretraining on 1M MOFs (graph plus energy grid); the foundation-model approach for porous materials.
45. [The Open DAC 2023 Dataset and Challenges for Sorbent Discovery in Direct Air Capture](https://doi.org/10.1021/acscentsci.3c01629) — Sriram et al., *ACS Central Science* 2024. Large DFT dataset of CO2/H2O adsorption in MOFs; extends the Open Catalyst approach to sorbents.
46. [MOFDiff: Coarse-grained Diffusion for Metal-Organic Framework Design](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.10732) — Fu et al., *ICLR* 2024. Coarse-grained building-block diffusion for generating MOFs for carbon capture.
47. [ChatGPT Chemistry Assistant for Text Mining and the Prediction of MOF Synthesis](https://doi.org/10.1021/jacs.3c05819) — Zheng et al., *Journal of the American Chemical Society* 2023. Prompt-engineered LLM extraction of MOF synthesis conditions from the literature, plus a predictor built on them.

## 6. Synthesis Prediction & Literature Text Mining
48. [Machine-learning-assisted materials discovery using failed experiments](https://doi.org/10.1038/nature17439) — Raccuglia et al., *Nature* 2016. Lab notebooks, including failed reactions, train a model that beats chemists' intuition for templated vanadium selenites; makes the case for negative data.
49. [Materials Synthesis Insights from Scientific Literature via Text Extraction and Machine Learning](https://doi.org/10.1021/acs.chemmater.7b03500) — Kim et al., *Chemistry of Materials* 2017. Pipeline that turns papers into synthesis parameters; the start of NLP for materials synthesis.
50. ⭐ [Unsupervised word embeddings capture latent knowledge from materials science literature](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-019-1335-8) — Tshitoyan et al., *Nature* 2019. word2vec on 3.3M abstracts "predicts" thermoelectrics years before they are reported; the founding paper for literature-as-data.
51. [Text-mined dataset of inorganic materials synthesis recipes](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41597-019-0224-1) — Kononova et al., *Scientific Data* 2019. About 19k solid-state recipes (precursors, operations, conditions); the main dataset for synthesis prediction.
52. [Predicting the synthesizability of crystalline inorganic materials from the data of known material compositions](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41524-023-01114-4) — Antoniuk et al., *npj Computational Materials* 2023. SynthNN: positive-unlabeled learning on ICSD compositions; outperforms hull energy as a synthesizability filter.
53. [Structured information extraction from scientific text with large language models](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-024-45563-x) — Dagdelen et al., *Nature Communications* 2024. Fine-tuned LLMs for joint named-entity and relation extraction into JSON schemas; the current best practice for text mining.
54. [Accurate prediction of synthesizability and precursors of 3D crystal structures via large language models](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-025-61778-y) — Song et al., *Nature Communications* 2025. CSLLM: three fine-tuned LLMs predicting synthesizability, synthesis method and precursors from a text encoding of the structure.

## 7. Characterization ML (XRD & Microscopy)
55. [Fast and interpretable classification of small X-ray diffraction datasets using data augmentation and deep neural networks](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41524-019-0196-x) — Oviedo et al., *npj Computational Materials* 2019. Physics-informed augmentation for classifying XRD with very little data; the basic recipe for diffraction ML.
56. [Machine learning in scanning transmission electron microscopy](https://doi.org/10.1038/s43586-022-00095-w) — Kalinin et al., *Nature Reviews Methods Primers* 2022. Primer on ML for STEM: atom finding, denoising, and autonomous microscopy.
57. [Ab initio structure solutions from nanocrystalline powder diffraction data via diffusion models](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41563-025-02220-y) — Guo et al., *Nature Materials* 2025. PXRDnet: a diffusion model that solves crystal structures from broadened powder patterns; generative models applied to characterization.

## 8. Autonomous / Self-Driving Labs
58. [Self-driving laboratory for accelerated discovery of thin-film materials](https://doi.org/10.1126/sciadv.aaz8867) — MacLeod et al., *Science Advances* 2020. Ada: a closed-loop robot with Bayesian optimization for thin-film hole-transport materials; the canonical small-scale self-driving lab.
59. [A mobile robotic chemist](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-020-2442-2) — Burger et al., *Nature* 2020. A mobile robot running 688 photocatalyst experiments over 8 days with batched Bayesian optimization.
60. [On-the-fly closed-loop materials discovery via Bayesian active learning](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-020-19597-w) — Kusne et al., *Nature Communications* 2020. CAMEO: autonomous phase mapping at a synchrotron that finds a new phase-change memory material.
61. ⭐ [An autonomous laboratory for the accelerated synthesis of inorganic materials](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-023-06734-w) — Szymanski et al., *Nature* 2023. A-Lab: robots, text-mined recipes, active learning and ML-XRD for solid-state synthesis. Also read the [2026 Author Correction](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-025-09992-y), which revises the claims to 36 of 57 targets, "new to the platform" rather than new to science.
62. ⭐ [Challenges in High-Throughput Inorganic Materials Prediction and Autonomous Synthesis](https://doi.org/10.1103/PRXEnergy.3.011002) — Leeman et al., *PRX Energy* 2024. The critique of A-Lab and GNoME: unreliable automated Rietveld analysis and ignored compositional disorder mean the "novel" materials are largely known.
63. [Self-Driving Laboratories for Chemistry and Materials Science](https://doi.org/10.1021/acs.chemrev.4c00055) — Tom et al., *Chemical Reviews* 2024. Comprehensive review of self-driving lab hardware, software, algorithms and autonomy levels.
64. ⭐ [A multimodal robotic platform for multi-element electrocatalyst discovery](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-025-09640-5) — Zhang et al., *Nature* 2025. CRESt (MIT): a multimodal-LLM copilot combined with robotic synthesis, electrochemistry and microscopy; more than 900 chemistries tested, ending in an 8-element formate fuel-cell catalyst.
65. [Agentic LLM Reasoning in a Self-Driving Laboratory for Air-Sensitive Lithium Halide Spinel Conductors](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.11957) — Fei et al., *arXiv* 2026. An LLM agent using abductive and inductive reasoning drives a glovebox A-Lab variant over 352 samples; the hit rate rises from about 1.3% to 5.3%.

## 9. LLMs, Agents & Foundation Models for Materials
66. [Leveraging large language models for predictive chemistry](https://doi.org/10.1038/s42256-023-00788-1) — Jablonka et al., *Nature Machine Intelligence* 2024. Fine-tuned GPT-3 on question-answer pairs matches dedicated models in the low-data regime, including for materials and MOF properties.
67. [Foundation models for materials discovery – current state and future directions](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41524-025-01538-0) — Pyzer-Knapp et al., *npj Computational Materials* 2025. Survey of foundation models (LLMs, structure models, multimodal) for materials and their open problems.
68. [Automating alloy design and discovery with physics-aware multimodal multiagent AI](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.2414074122) — Ghafarollahi & Buehler, *PNAS* 2025. AtomAgents: LLM agents that plan and run atomistic simulations and analyze results to design alloys.
69. [Autonomous in-silico inorganic materials discovery via multi-agent physics-aware scientific reasoning](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41524-026-02205-8) — Ghafarollahi & Buehler, *npj Computational Materials* 2026. SparksMatter: a multi-agent ideate–plan–execute–critique loop with materials tools (MP, generators, predictors) for thermoelectrics, semiconductors and perovskites.
70. [Towards Agentic Intelligence for Materials Science](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.00169) — Zhang et al., *arXiv* 2026. Roadmap survey of agentic materials discovery: LLM agents, tool use, closed loops and evaluation; a 2026 snapshot of the frontier.
