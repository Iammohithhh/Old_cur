---
title: "Machine-Learning Interatomic Potentials: reading list"
---

_Curated 2026-11. ⭐ = essential (read first). Links: DOI for journal papers, arXiv abs page otherwise._

## 1. Foundations: learning the potential energy surface
1. ⭐ [Generalized Neural-Network Representation of High-Dimensional Potential-Energy Surfaces](https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevLett.98.146401) — Behler & Parrinello, *Phys. Rev. Lett.* 2007. The founding idea: write total energy as a sum of atomic energies from local-environment NNs, which makes the model size-extensive and transferable.
2. ⭐ [Gaussian Approximation Potentials: The Accuracy of Quantum Mechanics, without the Electrons](https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevLett.104.136403) — Bartók et al., *Phys. Rev. Lett.* 2010. GAP: kernel/GP regression of atomic energies. Starts the kernel line of work that runs through SOAP to ACE.
3. [Machine learning of accurate energy-conserving molecular force fields](https://doi.org/10.1126/sciadv.1603015) — Chmiela et al., *Science Advances* 2017. GDML: learn forces directly in a gradient-domain kernel so they are energy-conserving by construction. Introduces the MD17 data.
4. [ANI-1: an extensible neural network potential with DFT accuracy at force field computational cost](https://doi.org/10.1039/C6SC05720A) — Smith, Isayev & Roitberg, *Chemical Science* 2017. Brings Behler-Parrinello NNs to transferable organic chemistry at scale (modified symmetry functions, millions of conformers).
5. [Deep Potential Molecular Dynamics: A Scalable Model with the Accuracy of Quantum Mechanics](https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevLett.120.143001) — Zhang et al., *Phys. Rev. Lett.* 2018. DeePMD: an end-to-end descriptor and fitting network. It became the base of the widely used DeePMD-kit/DP-GEN ecosystem.

## 2. Descriptors and body-ordered expansions
6. [Atom-centered symmetry functions for constructing high-dimensional neural network potentials](https://doi.org/10.1063/1.3553717) — Behler, *J. Chem. Phys.* 2011. The standard reference for ACSFs (radial and angular G-functions), the canonical hand-crafted invariant descriptor.
7. ⭐ [On representing chemical environments](https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevB.87.184115) — Bartók, Kondor & Csányi, *Phys. Rev. B* 2013. SOAP: smooth density-based power-spectrum descriptor. Essential for understanding the completeness and smoothness of invariants.
8. [Moment Tensor Potentials: A Class of Systematically Improvable Interatomic Potentials](https://doi.org/10.1137/15M1054183) — Shapeev, *Multiscale Model. Simul.* 2016. MTP: a polynomial basis of moment tensors that is systematically improvable, fast, and linear in its parameters.
9. ⭐ [Atomic cluster expansion for accurate and transferable interatomic potentials](https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevB.99.014104) — Drautz, *Phys. Rev. B* 2019. ACE: a complete body-ordered basis that unifies ACSF, SOAP, MTP and SNAP. It is the theoretical backbone behind MACE and GRACE.
10. [Incompleteness of Atomic Structure Representations](https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevLett.125.166001) — Pozdnyakov et al., *Phys. Rev. Lett.* 2020. A critical result: low-body-order invariants (including 3-body ones like SOAP) cannot tell certain distinct environments apart. This motivates higher body order.
11. [Physics-Inspired Structural Representations for Molecules and Materials](https://doi.org/10.1021/acs.chemrev.1c00021) — Musil et al., *Chem. Rev.* 2021. The definitive review of descriptors: symmetry, body order, completeness, and how they connect to equivariant NNs.

## 3. Message-passing and equivariant GNN potentials
12. [SchNet: A continuous-filter convolutional neural network for modeling quantum interactions](https://arxiv.org/abs/1706.08566) — Schütt et al., *NeurIPS* 2017. The first widely adopted learned-representation GNN potential, using continuous-filter convolutions on interatomic distances.
13. [Directional Message Passing for Molecular Graphs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2003.03123) — Gasteiger (Klicpera) et al., *ICLR* 2020. DimeNet: adds bond angles through directional (edge-based) messages and spherical Bessel bases.
14. [Equivariant message passing for the prediction of tensorial properties and molecular spectra](https://arxiv.org/abs/2102.03150) — Schütt et al., *ICML* 2021. PaiNN: cheap vector (l=1) equivariant features, still a strong, fast baseline.
15. [GemNet: Universal Directional Graph Neural Networks for Molecules](https://arxiv.org/abs/2106.08903) — Gasteiger et al., *NeurIPS* 2021. Two-hop and dihedral message passing. As GemNet-OC it was a workhorse of the OC20 era.
16. ⭐ [E(3)-equivariant graph neural networks for data-efficient and accurate interatomic potentials](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-022-29939-5) — Batzner et al., *Nat. Commun.* 2022. NequIP: tensor-product (e3nn) equivariant message passing. Its huge data-efficiency gains made equivariance the default.
17. ⭐ [MACE: Higher Order Equivariant Message Passing Neural Networks for Fast and Accurate Force Fields](https://arxiv.org/abs/2206.07697) — Batatia et al., *NeurIPS* 2022. Combines ACE-style many-body messages with equivariant MPNNs, so two layers are enough. This architecture underlies many foundation models.
18. [Learning local equivariant representations for large-scale atomistic dynamics](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-023-36329-y) — Musaelian et al., *Nat. Commun.* 2023. Allegro: a strictly local equivariant model that is parallelizable to millions of atoms. Shows the locality-versus-message-passing trade-off.
19. [The design space of E(3)-equivariant atom-centred interatomic potentials](https://doi.org/10.1038/s42256-024-00956-x) — Batatia et al., *Nat. Mach. Intell.* 2025. A unifying framework that places ACE, NequIP, MACE and others in one design space. Read it to understand *why* architectures work.
20. [EquiformerV2: Improved Equivariant Transformer for Scaling to Higher-Degree Representations](https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.12059) — Liao et al., *ICLR* 2024. An equivariant attention model using eSCN's SO(2) convolutions for high-l features. It held state-of-the-art on OC20 and is the ancestor of eSEN/UMA.
21. [Graph atomic cluster expansion for semilocal interactions beyond equivariant message passing](https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevX.14.021036) — Bochkarev, Lysogorskiy & Drautz, *Phys. Rev. X* 2024. GRACE: extends ACE to graph (tree) basis functions, giving a complete semilocal basis. This is the basis of the GRACE foundation models.
22. [The dark side of the forces: assessing non-conservative force models for atomistic machine learning](https://arxiv.org/abs/2412.11569) — Bigi, Langer & Ceriotti, *ICML* 2025. A critical analysis: direct (non-conservative) force heads break geometry optimization and MD. It proposes hybrid and fine-tuning fixes.

## 4. Long-range electrostatics, charges and non-locality
23. [PhysNet: A Neural Network for Predicting Energies, Forces, Dipole Moments, and Partial Charges](https://doi.org/10.1021/acs.jctc.9b00181) — Unke & Meuwly, *J. Chem. Theory Comput.* 2019. A message-passing potential with learned charges, explicit Coulomb terms and D3 dispersion. A template for many later physics-augmented models.
24. [Incorporating long-range physics in atomic-scale machine learning](https://doi.org/10.1063/1.5128375) — Grisafi & Ceriotti, *J. Chem. Phys.* 2019. LODE: long-distance equivariant descriptors built from atom-density potentials, which carry 1/r physics into local features.
25. ⭐ [A fourth-generation high-dimensional neural network potential with accurate electrostatics including non-local charge transfer](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-020-20427-2) — Ko et al., *Nat. Commun.* 2021. 4G-HDNNP: charge equilibration with a global charge, which fixes failures of local models under non-local charge transfer.
26. [Ewald-based Long-Range Message Passing for Molecular Graphs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.04791) — Kosmala et al., *ICML* 2023. A reciprocal-space (frequency-cutoff) message-passing add-on that gives any GNN long-range interactions.
27. [Latent Ewald summation for machine learning of long-range interactions](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41524-025-01577-7) — Cheng, *npj Comput. Mater.* 2025. LES: learn latent charges from local features and Ewald-sum them, trained on energies and forces only, at about 2x the cost of a short-range model.
28. [Machine learning of charges and long-range interactions from energies and forces](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-025-63852-x) — King et al., *Nat. Commun.* 2025. Shows that LES latent charges recover physical partial charges and dipoles, beating models trained explicitly on charges. (2025 landmark.)
29. [AIMNet2: a neural network potential to meet your neutral, charged, organic, and elemental-organic needs](https://doi.org/10.1039/D4SC08572H) — Anstine, Zubatyuk & Isayev, *Chemical Science* 2025. A production molecular potential with charge and spin handling, explicit electrostatics and dispersion across 14 elements.

## 5. Universal / foundation potentials
30. ⭐ [A universal graph deep learning interatomic potential for the periodic table](https://doi.org/10.1038/s43588-022-00349-3) — Chen & Ong, *Nat. Comput. Sci.* 2022. M3GNet: the first widely used universal potential, trained on Materials Project relaxation trajectories.
31. [CHGNet as a pretrained universal neural network potential for charge-informed atomistic modelling](https://doi.org/10.1038/s42256-023-00716-3) — Deng et al., *Nat. Mach. Intell.* 2023. Introduces the MPtrj dataset and uses magnetic moments as a proxy for charge state.
32. ⭐ [A foundation model for atomistic materials chemistry](https://doi.org/10.1063/5.0297006) — Batatia et al., *J. Chem. Phys.* 2025 (arXiv 2401.00096). MACE-MP-0: one MPtrj-trained model that runs stable MD across dozens of diverse applications. It defined the "foundation model plus fine-tune" paradigm.
33. [MACE-OFF: Short-Range Transferable Machine Learning Force Fields for Organic Molecules](https://doi.org/10.1021/jacs.4c07099) — Kovács et al., *J. Am. Chem. Soc.* 2025. Transferable organic and biomolecular MACE force fields trained on SPICE, validated on condensed-phase properties.
34. [MatterSim: A Deep Learning Atomistic Model Across Elements, Temperatures and Pressures](https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.04967) — Yang et al., arXiv 2024. Microsoft's universal model trained on actively sampled data spanning 0–5000 K and up to 1000 GPa. Covers free energies and fine-tuning efficiency.
35. [DPA-2: a large atomic model as a multi-task learner](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41524-024-01493-2) — Zhang et al., *npj Comput. Mater.* 2024. Multi-task pre-training across heterogeneous DFT datasets with separate heads, the DeePMD route to large atomic models.
36. [Orb-v3: atomistic simulation at scale](https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.06231) — Rhodes et al., arXiv 2025. A non-equivariant, speed-optimized universal model family (built on Orb's diffusion pre-training) that pushes the speed/memory/accuracy Pareto front, with conservative and direct variants.
37. [Learning Smooth and Expressive Interatomic Potentials for Physical Property Prediction](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.12147) — Fu et al., *ICML* 2025. eSEN: shows that smoothness and energy conservation, not test MAE alone, predict phonon and thermal-conductivity accuracy. State-of-the-art on Matbench Discovery with OMat24.
38. ⭐ [UMA: A Family of Universal Models for Atoms](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.23971) — Wood et al., *NeurIPS* 2025. A mixture-of-linear-experts model trained on about 500M structures (OMat24, OMol25, OC20, ODAC and more) that covers molecules, materials and catalysts with one model. (2025 landmark.)
39. [PET-MAD as a lightweight universal interatomic potential for advanced materials modeling](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-025-65662-7) — Mazitov et al., *Nat. Commun.* 2025. An unconstrained Point-Edge-Transformer trained on a small but maximally diverse dataset (MAD). Shows that data diversity can beat dataset size.
40. [Optimizing cross-domain transfer for universal machine learning interatomic potentials](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-026-70195-8) — Kim et al., *Nat. Commun.* 2026 (arXiv 2510.11241). SevenNet-Omni: multi-fidelity training on 15 datasets with a small "domain-bridging set" that aligns inconsistent DFT references. (2026 landmark.)
41. [Fast and Accurate Equivariant Foundation Models for Atomistic Simulation](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.28461) — Kavanagh et al., arXiv 2026. NequIP and Allegro foundation models with compiled training and inference. Argues that a top-tier foundation model now costs only a few hundred GPU-hours. (2026.)

## 6. Datasets
42. [On the role of gradients for machine learning of molecular energies and forces](https://doi.org/10.1088/2632-2153/abba6f) — Christensen & von Lilienfeld, *Mach. Learn.: Sci. Technol.* 2020. Introduces revised MD17 (rMD17) after showing that the original MD17 labels are noisy. Use it to understand the benchmark everyone quotes.
43. [The ANI-1ccx and ANI-1x data sets, coupled-cluster and density functional theory properties for molecules](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41597-020-0473-z) — Smith et al., *Scientific Data* 2020. An active-learning-curated organic dataset with a CCSD(T)/CBS subset, the classic transfer-learning resource.
44. [SPICE, A Dataset of Drug-like Molecules and Peptides for Training Machine Learning Potentials](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41597-022-01882-6) — Eastman et al., *Scientific Data* 2023. ωB97M-D3BJ data covering drug-like molecules, peptides, ions and dimers. Training set for MACE-OFF and many biomolecular models.
45. [Open Catalyst 2020 (OC20) Dataset and Community Challenges](https://doi.org/10.1021/acscatal.0c04525) — Chanussot et al., *ACS Catalysis* 2021. A 1.3M-relaxation adsorbate–surface dataset (plus OC22 for oxides). It drove the scaling of GNN architectures (GemNet, Equiformer, eSCN).
46. [The Open DAC 2023 Dataset and Challenges for Sorbent Discovery in Direct Air Capture](https://doi.org/10.1021/acscentsci.3c01629) — Sriram et al., *ACS Central Science* 2024. ODAC23: 38M+ DFT calculations of CO2/H2O adsorption in MOFs, a porous-framework domain for MLIPs.
47. [Improving machine-learning models in materials science through large datasets](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.mtphys.2024.101560) — Schmidt et al., *Materials Today Physics* 2024. The Alexandria database (5M+ DFT calculations). Its relaxation trajectories now accompany MPtrj in training universal potentials.
48. [Open Materials 2024 (OMat24) Inorganic Materials Dataset and Models](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.12771) — Barroso-Luque et al., arXiv 2024. 110M non-equilibrium (rattled, AIMD, high-T) DFT calculations. Its far-from-equilibrium sampling largely addressed PES softening.
49. ⭐ [The Open Molecules 2025 (OMol25) Dataset, Evaluations, and Models](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.08762) — Levine et al., arXiv 2025. More than 100M ωB97M-V/def2-TZVPD calculations spanning biomolecules, electrolytes and metal complexes up to 350 atoms. The new molecular reference dataset. (2025 landmark.)
50. [A Foundational Potential Energy Surface Dataset for Materials](https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.04070) — Kaplan et al., arXiv 2025. MatPES: about 400k carefully sampled structures with PBE and r2SCAN labels. Argues for data quality and diversity over volume.

## 7. Benchmarks and failure analyses
51. ⭐ [Forces are not Enough: Benchmark and Critical Evaluation for Machine Learning Force Fields with Molecular Simulations](https://arxiv.org/abs/2210.07237) — Fu et al., *TMLR* 2023. Low force MAE does not imply stable or accurate MD. Introduces simulation-based metrics such as stability and RDFs, and is required reading for evaluation.
52. ⭐ [A framework to evaluate machine learning crystal stability predictions](https://doi.org/10.1038/s42256-025-01055-1) — Riebesell et al., *Nat. Mach. Intell.* 2025. Matbench Discovery: a prospective stability-classification benchmark (F1 and discovery metrics, with κ_SRME later added to the leaderboard) that showed universal potentials winning. It is the de facto leaderboard.
53. [Systematic softening in universal machine learning interatomic potentials](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41524-024-01500-6) — Deng et al., *npj Comput. Mater.* 2025. Universal potentials systematically underestimate PES curvature (surfaces, defects, phonons, barriers) because training data is biased toward near-equilibrium structures. The error is largely correctable with a little fine-tuning.
54. [Universal machine learning interatomic potentials are ready for phonons](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41524-025-01650-1) — Loew et al., *npj Comput. Mater.* 2025. A benchmark of about 10k ab initio phonon calculations: some universal potentials are excellent, others fail despite good energy and force errors.
55. [MLIP Arena: Advancing Fairness and Transparency in Machine Learning Interatomic Potentials via an Open, Accessible Benchmark Platform](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.20630) — Chiang et al., *NeurIPS (Datasets & Benchmarks)* 2025. Physics-aware tests (diatomic curves, extreme-condition MD, reactivity) that are free of DFT-reference leakage.
56. [Amorphous materials as a frontier challenge for universal interatomic potentials](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.11384) — Fragapane & Deringer, arXiv 2026. A systematic benchmark showing that many pre-trained models transfer poorly to disordered solids, and which fine-tuning strategies help. (2026.)

## 8. Active learning, uncertainty, fine-tuning and distillation
57. [Active learning of linearly parametrized interatomic potentials](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.commatsci.2017.08.031) — Podryabinkin & Shapeev, *Comput. Mater. Sci.* 2017. D-optimality / MaxVol extrapolation grade for on-the-fly data selection, still used with MTP and ACE.
58. [Committee neural network potentials control generalization errors and enable active learning](https://doi.org/10.1063/5.0016004) — Schran, Brezina & Marsalek, *J. Chem. Phys.* 2020. Query-by-committee for NN potentials: ensemble disagreement as both an error estimate and a selection criterion.
59. [DP-GEN: A concurrent learning platform for the generation of reliable deep learning based potential energy models](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cpc.2020.107206) — Zhang et al., *Comput. Phys. Commun.* 2020. The standard explore → label → train loop driven by model deviation.
60. [On-the-fly active learning of interpretable Bayesian force fields for atomistic rare events](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41524-020-0283-z) — Vandermause et al., *npj Comput. Mater.* 2020. FLARE: GP force fields with built-in uncertainty that trigger DFT calls during MD.
61. [Single-model uncertainty quantification in neural network potentials does not consistently outperform model ensembles](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41524-023-01180-8) — Tan et al., *npj Comput. Mater.* 2023. A negative result: mean-variance, evidential and GMM single-model UQ do not reliably beat ensembles.
62. [Fine-tuning foundation models of materials interatomic potentials with frozen transfer learning](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41524-025-01727-x) — Radova et al., *npj Comput. Mater.* 2025. Partially frozen MACE-MP fine-tuning reaches chemical accuracy with a few hundred structures. A practical recipe.
63. [Towards Fast, Specialized Machine Learning Force Fields: Distilling Foundation Models via Energy Hessians](https://arxiv.org/abs/2501.09009) — Amin, Raja & Krishnapriyan, *ICLR* 2025. Hessian-matching distillation of large foundation models into small students that are up to 20x faster.
64. [Distillation of atomistic foundation models across architectures and chemical domains](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.10956) — Gardner et al., arXiv 2025. An architecture-agnostic synthetic-data distillation protocol (MACE-MP into PaiNN, TensorNet or ACE) for fast domain-specific potentials.

## 9. Reviews and perspectives
65. [Four Generations of High-Dimensional Neural Network Potentials](https://doi.org/10.1021/acs.chemrev.0c00868) — Behler, *Chem. Rev.* 2021. A taxonomy (1G to 4G) of NN potentials by how they treat locality and long-range interactions.
66. ⭐ [Machine Learning Force Fields](https://doi.org/10.1021/acs.chemrev.0c01111) — Unke et al., *Chem. Rev.* 2021. The best single entry-point review: theory, descriptors, kernels versus NNs, and best practices.
67. [A practical guide to machine learning interatomic potentials – Status and future](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cossms.2025.101214) — Jacobs et al., *Curr. Opin. Solid State Mater. Sci.* 2025. A pragmatic 2025 guide to choosing, training and validating MLIPs, including universal ones.
68. [Uncertainty in the era of machine learning for atomistic modeling](https://doi.org/10.1039/D5DD00102A) — Grasselli et al., *Digital Discovery* 2025. A perspective on UQ for MLIPs: sources of error, calibration, and propagation to observables.
69. [Foundation models for atomistic simulation of chemistry and materials](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41570-025-00793-5) — Yuan et al., *Nat. Rev. Chem.* 2026 (arXiv 2503.10538). Defines "foundation" versus "universal" potentials and surveys data, architectures and fine-tuning.
70. [Six Open Questions in Machine-Learned Interatomic Potential Foundation Models](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.07327) — Creed et al., arXiv 2026. A community perspective setting the research agenda (definition, data, long-range interactions, evaluation, fidelity, efficiency) for foundational MLIPs. (2026.)

## Learning path
**Stage 1: Foundations (about 2–3 weeks).** 66 → 1 → 6 → 2 → 7 → 8 → 9 → 10 → 3 → 4 → 5 → 65 → 11. You will understand locality, invariant descriptors, kernel versus NN regression, body order and completeness, and why forces come from energy gradients.

**Stage 2: Core architectures, data and evaluation (about 3–4 weeks).** 12 → 13 → 14 → 16 → 17 → 18 → 19 → 21 → 15 → 20, then long-range 23 → 24 → 25 → 27 → 28. Datasets: 42 → 43 → 44 → 45. Evaluation: 51 → 22. Active learning and UQ: 57 → 58 → 59 → 60 → 61. By the end you should be able to train a NequIP or MACE model, run active learning, and judge it by MD stability rather than MAE alone.

**Stage 3: Frontier — foundation models and their limits (ongoing).** 30 → 31 → 32 → 52 → 53 → 48 → 37 → 38 → 49 → 33 → 34 → 35 → 36 → 39 → 40 → 41 → 47 → 50 → 46 → 26 → 29, then reliability and adaptation 54 → 55 → 56 → 62 → 63 → 64, and finish with the big-picture perspectives 67 → 68 → 69 → 70.
