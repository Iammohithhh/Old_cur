title: "Core AI for AI4Science: reading list"
---

_Curated 2026-11. ⭐ = essential (read first). Links: DOI for journal papers, arXiv abs page otherwise._

Scope: the general-purpose deep learning toolkit that modern MLIPs, generative materials/molecule models and scientific LLM agents are built from. Domain-specific models (MACE, NequIP, MatterGen, etc.), PINNs/neural operators and biology are covered in sibling lists.

## 1. Deep Learning Foundations & Optimization
1. [ImageNet Classification with Deep Convolutional Neural Networks](https://doi.org/10.1145/3065386) — Krizhevsky et al., *NeurIPS* 2012 (CACM reprint 2017). The GPU + ReLU + dropout + big-data recipe that started the deep learning era; read it for historical context.
2. ⭐ [Deep Residual Learning for Image Recognition](https://arxiv.org/abs/1512.03385) — He et al., *CVPR* 2016. Residual connections make very deep networks trainable; every message-passing MLIP and transformer stacks residual blocks.
3. [Batch Normalization: Accelerating Deep Network Training by Reducing Internal Covariate Shift](https://arxiv.org/abs/1502.03167) — Ioffe & Szegedy, *ICML* 2015. Normalization as a training stabilizer; also shows why batch statistics are awkward for variable-size graphs and atoms.
4. [Layer Normalization](https://arxiv.org/abs/1607.06450) — Ba et al., arXiv 2016. Per-sample normalization used in every transformer and most graph/equivariant networks (with equivariant variants for vector features).
5. ⭐ [Adam: A Method for Stochastic Optimization](https://arxiv.org/abs/1412.6980) — Kingma & Ba, *ICLR* 2015. The default optimizer for nearly all scientific ML; understand its moments and bias correction before tuning MLIP training.
6. [Transformers without Normalization](https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.10622) — Zhu et al., *CVPR* 2025. Replaces LayerNorm with an element-wise Dynamic Tanh; a 2025 reminder that "standard" architecture components are still being questioned and simplified.

## 2. Sequence Models, Transformers & Scaling Laws
7. ⭐ [Attention Is All You Need](https://arxiv.org/abs/1706.03762) — Vaswani et al., *NeurIPS* 2017. The transformer: self-attention as learned, permutation-equivariant message passing on a complete graph. The backbone of LLMs, AlphaFold-style trunks and many atomistic models.
8. [BERT: Pre-training of Deep Bidirectional Transformers for Language Understanding](https://arxiv.org/abs/1810.04805) — Devlin et al., *NAACL* 2019. Masked-token pretraining then fine-tuning; the template for SMILES/sequence/crystal "foundation model" pretraining.
9. [Language Models are Few-Shot Learners (GPT-3)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2005.14165) — Brown et al., *NeurIPS* 2020. Scale yields in-context learning; the starting point for LLMs as general scientific assistants.
10. [An Image is Worth 16x16 Words: Transformers for Image Recognition at Scale (ViT)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2010.11929) — Dosovitskiy et al., *ICLR* 2021. Patch tokenization shows transformers win with weak inductive biases and enough data — the same tension as in the equivariance debate (Sec. 4).
11. [FlashAttention: Fast and Memory-Efficient Exact Attention with IO-Awareness](https://arxiv.org/abs/2205.14135) — Dao et al., *NeurIPS* 2022. IO-aware kernels make long-context attention practical; essential systems thinking for large atomistic/sequence models.
12. ⭐ [Scaling Laws for Neural Language Models](https://arxiv.org/abs/2001.08361) — Kaplan et al., arXiv 2020. Loss falls as a power law in parameters, data and compute; the framework now used to study scaling of MLIPs and materials foundation models.
13. [Training Compute-Optimal Large Language Models (Chinchilla)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2203.15556) — Hoffmann et al., *NeurIPS* 2022. Corrects Kaplan: scale data and parameters together. Shows how to allocate a fixed compute budget, a key question when training on expensive DFT data.

## 3. Graph Neural Networks
14. [Relational Inductive Biases, Deep Learning, and Graph Networks](https://arxiv.org/abs/1806.01261) — Battaglia et al., arXiv 2018. A unified "graph network" view (edge, node and global updates) that covers most physical-simulation GNNs.
15. ⭐ [Neural Message Passing for Quantum Chemistry](https://arxiv.org/abs/1704.01212) — Gilmer et al., *ICML* 2017. Introduces the MPNN framework, tested on QM9; the conceptual root of all molecular GNNs and MLIPs.
16. [Semi-Supervised Classification with Graph Convolutional Networks](https://arxiv.org/abs/1609.02907) — Kipf & Welling, *ICLR* 2017. The GCN: a spectral approximation reduced to simple neighbourhood averaging; the baseline everyone compares to.
17. [Inductive Representation Learning on Large Graphs (GraphSAGE)](https://arxiv.org/abs/1706.02216) — Hamilton et al., *NeurIPS* 2017. Neighbour sampling and aggregators that generalize to unseen graphs; inductive learning is the default setting in chemistry.
18. [Graph Attention Networks](https://arxiv.org/abs/1710.10903) — Veličković et al., *ICLR* 2018. Attention-weighted aggregation; a precursor of graph transformers and of attention-based equivariant models.
19. [How Powerful are Graph Neural Networks? (GIN)](https://arxiv.org/abs/1810.00826) — Xu et al., *ICLR* 2019. Ties message passing to the Weisfeiler–Leman test; the standard language for GNN expressivity (extended to geometric graphs in #31).
20. [Deeper Insights into Graph Convolutional Networks for Semi-Supervised Learning](https://arxiv.org/abs/1801.07606) — Li et al., *AAAI* 2018. First clear analysis of oversmoothing (GCN as Laplacian smoothing); explains why naively deep GNNs degrade.
21. [On the Bottleneck of Graph Neural Networks and its Practical Implications](https://arxiv.org/abs/2006.05205) — Alon & Yahav, *ICLR* 2021. Names oversquashing: long-range information gets compressed through bottlenecks. This motivates cutoff, long-range and electrostatics choices in MLIPs.

## 4. Geometric Deep Learning & Equivariance
22. ⭐ [Geometric Deep Learning: Grids, Groups, Graphs, Geodesics, and Gauges](https://arxiv.org/abs/2104.13478) — Bronstein et al., arXiv 2021. The unifying "symmetry + scale separation" blueprint; read it to see CNNs, GNNs, transformers and equivariant nets as one family.
23. [Group Equivariant Convolutional Networks](https://arxiv.org/abs/1602.07576) — Cohen & Welling, *ICML* 2016. The founding paper on building group symmetry into layers by construction.
24. ⭐ [Tensor Field Networks: Rotation- and Translation-Equivariant Neural Networks for 3D Point Clouds](https://arxiv.org/abs/1802.08219) — Thomas et al., arXiv 2018. Spherical harmonics + Clebsch–Gordan tensor products on point clouds. This is the direct ancestor of NequIP, MACE and Allegro.
25. [SE(3)-Transformers: 3D Roto-Translation Equivariant Attention Networks](https://arxiv.org/abs/2006.10503) — Fuchs et al., *NeurIPS* 2020. Adds attention to TFN-style equivariant features; the template for Equiformer-type models.
26. ⭐ [E(n) Equivariant Graph Neural Networks](https://arxiv.org/abs/2102.09844) — Satorras et al., *ICML* 2021. Simple, cheap equivariance by updating coordinates with scalar-weighted relative vectors; widely used in molecular generative models.
27. [e3nn: Euclidean Neural Networks](https://arxiv.org/abs/2207.09453) — Geiger & Smidt, arXiv 2022. Practical guide and library for irreps, spherical harmonics and tensor products; essential reading before implementing or modifying an equivariant MLIP.
28. [Scalars are Universal: Equivariant Machine Learning, Structured like Classical Physics](https://arxiv.org/abs/2106.06610) — Villar et al., *NeurIPS* 2021. Proves that invariant scalars (dot products, norms) are enough to build universal equivariant functions; the theory behind scalarization-based models.
29. [Frame Averaging for Invariant and Equivariant Network Design](https://arxiv.org/abs/2110.03336) — Puny et al., *ICLR* 2022. Makes any backbone equivariant by averaging over a small, data-dependent set of frames; the main alternative to equivariant-by-construction layers.
30. [Reducing SO(3) Convolutions to SO(2) for Efficient Equivariant GNNs (eSCN)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2302.03655) — Passaro & Zitnick, *ICML* 2023. Aligning each edge to the z-axis cuts tensor-product cost from O(L⁶) to O(L³); the trick that made high-degree equivariant models (EquiformerV2, UMA-class) scale.
31. [On the Expressive Power of Geometric Graph Neural Networks](https://arxiv.org/abs/2301.09308) — Joshi et al., *ICML* 2023. A geometric WL test: what invariant and equivariant GNNs can and cannot tell apart, and why body order and tensor rank matter.
32. [Does Equivariance Matter at Scale?](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.23179) — Brehmer et al., *TMLR* 2025. Controlled scaling study: data augmentation closes most of the data-efficiency gap, yet equivariant models stay more compute-efficient. Central to the 2024–26 "do we need equivariance?" debate.

## 5. Generative Models: VAEs, Flows, Diffusion & Flow Matching
33. ⭐ [Auto-Encoding Variational Bayes](https://arxiv.org/abs/1312.6114) — Kingma & Welling, *ICLR* 2014. The VAE and the reparameterization trick; the ELBO view underlies latent generative models and amortized inference in science.
34. [Generative Adversarial Networks](https://arxiv.org/abs/1406.2661) — Goodfellow et al., *NeurIPS* 2014. Adversarial training as implicit density modelling; largely replaced by diffusion in science, but the concepts recur in many places.
35. [Neural Ordinary Differential Equations](https://arxiv.org/abs/1806.07366) — Chen et al., *NeurIPS* 2018. Continuous-depth networks and continuous normalizing flows (CNFs) with adjoint gradients; the mathematical bridge to flow matching and physics-style dynamics.
36. ⭐ [Denoising Diffusion Probabilistic Models](https://arxiv.org/abs/2006.11239) — Ho et al., *NeurIPS* 2020. The DDPM noise-prediction objective that made diffusion practical; the basis of most structure-generation models (crystals, molecules, proteins).
37. ⭐ [Score-Based Generative Modeling through Stochastic Differential Equations](https://arxiv.org/abs/2011.13456) — Song et al., *ICLR* 2021. Unifies score matching and diffusion as forward/reverse SDEs plus the probability-flow ODE. The continuous-time view you need for designing samplers and noise processes.
38. [Denoising Diffusion Implicit Models (DDIM)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2010.02502) — Song et al., *ICLR* 2021. Deterministic, non-Markovian sampling with far fewer steps; the first step toward fast samplers.
39. [Classifier-Free Diffusion Guidance](https://arxiv.org/abs/2207.12598) — Ho & Salimans, *NeurIPS 2021 Workshop on Deep Generative Models*. Conditional generation without a separate classifier. This is how property-, composition- or symmetry-conditioned generators are steered.
40. [Elucidating the Design Space of Diffusion-Based Generative Models (EDM)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2206.00364) — Karras et al., *NeurIPS* 2022. Separates noise schedule, preconditioning and sampler choices cleanly; the practical reference adopted by AlphaFold 3-style diffusion modules.
41. [High-Resolution Image Synthesis with Latent Diffusion Models](https://arxiv.org/abs/2112.10752) — Rombach et al., *CVPR* 2022. Diffuse in a learned latent space with cross-attention conditioning; increasingly used for latent molecule/crystal generation.
42. [Scalable Diffusion Models with Transformers (DiT)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2212.09748) — Peebles & Xie, *ICCV* 2023. Transformer denoisers with adaptive-LayerNorm conditioning that scale predictably; the architecture pattern behind all-atom diffusion transformers.
43. ⭐ [Flow Matching for Generative Modeling](https://arxiv.org/abs/2210.02747) — Lipman et al., *ICLR* 2023. Simulation-free regression of a velocity field along probability paths. Now the default for many materials/molecule generators.
44. [Flow Straight and Fast: Learning to Generate and Transfer Data with Rectified Flow](https://arxiv.org/abs/2209.03003) — Liu et al., *ICLR* 2023. Straight-line interpolation paths plus "reflow" for few-step generation; developed alongside flow matching.
45. [Stochastic Interpolants: A Unifying Framework for Flows and Diffusions](https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.08797) — Albergo, Boffi & Vanden-Eijnden, *JMLR* 2025. A rigorous framework covering flows, diffusions and Schrödinger bridges between arbitrary distributions; useful for data-to-data transport problems in physics.
46. [Flow Matching on General Geometries (Riemannian Flow Matching)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2302.03790) — Chen & Lipman, *ICLR* 2024. Flow matching on manifolds (tori, spheres, SO(3)). This is what lets models generate periodic fractional coordinates and rotations.
47. [Consistency Models](https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.01469) — Song et al., *ICML* 2023. Learn to map any point on the trajectory directly to data for one- or few-step sampling; matters when generation feeds expensive screening loops.
48. [Discrete Diffusion Modeling by Estimating the Ratios of the Data Distribution (SEDD)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.16834) — Lou et al., *ICML* 2024. Score entropy for discrete data (ICML best paper); the basis of discrete diffusion for atom types, sequences and text.
49. [Mean Flows for One-step Generative Modeling](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.13447) — Geng et al., *NeurIPS* 2025. Models average rather than instantaneous velocity, giving strong one-step generation from scratch. A 2025 landmark in few-step flow models.
50. [Why Diffusion Models Don't Memorize: The Role of Implicit Dynamical Regularization in Training](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.17638) — Bonnaire et al., *NeurIPS* 2025. Shows separate timescales for generalization and memorization (NeurIPS 2025 best paper); directly relevant to novelty claims for generated materials.

## 6. Self-Supervised & Representation Learning
51. [Representation Learning with Contrastive Predictive Coding](https://arxiv.org/abs/1807.03748) — van den Oord et al., arXiv 2018. Introduces the InfoNCE loss, the mathematical core of contrastive learning.
52. [A Simple Framework for Contrastive Learning of Visual Representations (SimCLR)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2002.05709) — Chen et al., *ICML* 2020. Augmentations + projection head + large batches; the template for contrastive pretraining on molecules and crystals.
53. [Learning Transferable Visual Models From Natural Language Supervision (CLIP)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2103.00020) — Radford et al., *ICML* 2021. Cross-modal contrastive alignment; the model for structure–text and structure–spectrum joint embeddings.
54. [Masked Autoencoders Are Scalable Vision Learners](https://arxiv.org/abs/2111.06377) — He et al., *CVPR* 2022. High-ratio masking and reconstruction as a scalable pretext task; the counterpart of denoising pretraining on 3D structures.
55. [DINOv2: Learning Robust Visual Features without Supervision](https://arxiv.org/abs/2304.07193) — Oquab et al., *TMLR* 2024. Self-distillation at scale produces general frozen features; a reference point for building "foundation" representations of scientific data.

## 7. LLMs, Reasoning & Agents for Science
56. ⭐ [Training Language Models to Follow Instructions with Human Feedback (InstructGPT)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2203.02155) — Ouyang et al., *NeurIPS* 2022. SFT + reward model + PPO (RLHF); the post-training pipeline behind every assistant-style LLM.
57. [Direct Preference Optimization: Your Language Model is Secretly a Reward Model](https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.18290) — Rafailov et al., *NeurIPS* 2023. RLHF without an explicit reward model or RL loop; widely used to align domain LLMs and generators to preferences.
58. [Chain-of-Thought Prompting Elicits Reasoning in Large Language Models](https://arxiv.org/abs/2201.11903) — Wei et al., *NeurIPS* 2022. Intermediate reasoning steps unlock multi-step problem solving; the seed of today's reasoning models.
59. [Retrieval-Augmented Generation for Knowledge-Intensive NLP Tasks](https://arxiv.org/abs/2005.11401) — Lewis et al., *NeurIPS* 2020. Grounds generation in retrieved documents; the standard way to connect LLMs to literature and materials databases.
60. [ReAct: Synergizing Reasoning and Acting in Language Models](https://arxiv.org/abs/2210.03629) — Yao et al., *ICLR* 2023. Alternates reasoning traces with tool actions; the basic loop of LLM agents that run simulations and lab tools.
61. [Toolformer: Language Models Can Teach Themselves to Use Tools](https://arxiv.org/abs/2302.04761) — Schick et al., *NeurIPS* 2023. Self-supervised learning of API calls; the foundation for LLMs calling calculators, DFT codes and databases.
62. [Scaling LLM Test-Time Compute Optimally Can be More Effective than Scaling Parameters for Reasoning](https://arxiv.org/abs/2408.03314) — Snell et al., *ICLR* 2025. Compute-optimal use of search and verifiers at inference time; the analytical basis of "think longer" reasoning models.
63. ⭐ [DeepSeek-R1 Incentivizes Reasoning in LLMs through Reinforcement Learning](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-025-09422-z) — Guo et al., *Nature* 2025 ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2501.12948)). Shows large-scale RL with verifiable rewards (GRPO) produces long chain-of-thought reasoning; the open blueprint for reasoning models in math, code and science.
64. [Does Reinforcement Learning Really Incentivize Reasoning Capacity in LLMs Beyond the Base Model?](https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.13837) — Yue et al., *NeurIPS* 2025. Pass@k analysis suggests RLVR mostly sharpens sampling within what the base model can already do (NeurIPS 2025 runner-up). An essential counterpoint before training a "scientific reasoner".
65. [Large Language Diffusion Models (LLaDA)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.09992) — Nie et al., *NeurIPS* 2025. An 8B masked-diffusion LM competitive with autoregressive LLMs; shows discrete diffusion scales, which matters for sequence and crystal-string generation.
66. [AlphaEvolve: A Coding Agent for Scientific and Algorithmic Discovery](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.13131) — Novikov et al., arXiv 2025. LLM-driven evolutionary program search with automated evaluators finds new algorithms and math results; the template for evaluator-in-the-loop discovery.

## 8. Uncertainty Quantification & Bayesian Optimization
67. [Gaussian Processes for Machine Learning](https://doi.org/10.7551/mitpress/3206.001.0001) — Rasmussen & Williams, *MIT Press* 2006 (book; free at gaussianprocess.org/gpml). The reference for kernels, marginal likelihood and GP regression; the base of GAP-style potentials and Bayesian optimization.
68. [Dropout as a Bayesian Approximation: Representing Model Uncertainty in Deep Learning](https://arxiv.org/abs/1506.02142) — Gal & Ghahramani, *ICML* 2016. MC dropout as approximate variational inference; a cheap uncertainty baseline.
69. ⭐ [Simple and Scalable Predictive Uncertainty Estimation using Deep Ensembles](https://arxiv.org/abs/1612.01474) — Lakshminarayanan et al., *NeurIPS* 2017. Ensembles are the strongest practical UQ baseline; the workhorse for active learning of MLIPs.
70. [A Gentle Introduction to Conformal Prediction and Distribution-Free Uncertainty Quantification](https://arxiv.org/abs/2107.07511) — Angelopoulos & Bates, arXiv 2021. Distribution-free, finite-sample coverage guarantees; turns any model's errors into calibrated intervals for screening decisions.
71. [Practical Bayesian Optimization of Machine Learning Algorithms](https://arxiv.org/abs/1206.2944) — Snoek et al., *NeurIPS* 2012. GP-based BO with acquisition functions made practical; the template for closed-loop experiment and simulation design.
72. [BoTorch: A Framework for Efficient Monte-Carlo Bayesian Optimization](https://arxiv.org/abs/1910.06403) — Balandat et al., *NeurIPS* 2020. Modern MC acquisition functions (batch, multi-objective) and the library most self-driving-lab BO stacks build on.

## Learning path
**Stage 1 — Foundations (weeks 1–3):** 2 → 5 → 3 → 4 → 7 → 12 → 13 → 15 → 16 → 19 → 33 → 36 → 67 → 69. Get fluent in residual nets, optimization, attention, scaling, message passing, the VAE/ELBO, DDPM, GPs and ensembles. Skim 1, 8–10, 14, 17–18 for context.

**Stage 2 — Core methods for scientific modelling (weeks 4–9):** Geometry: 22 → 23 → 24 → 25 → 26 → 27 → 28 → 29 → 31 → 20 → 21. Generative: 35 → 37 → 38 → 39 → 40 → 43 → 44 → 46 → 41 → 42 → 47 → 48. Representation and UQ/BO: 51 → 52 → 54 → 68 → 70 → 71 → 72. LLMs: 56 → 58 → 59 → 60 → 61 → 57.

**Stage 3 — Frontier (weeks 10+):** 30 → 32 (efficient and "optional" equivariance), 45 (unified transport), 49 → 50 (one-step generation and memorization vs. novelty), 11 → 6 (efficient, simplified architectures), 53 → 55 (multimodal foundation representations), 62 → 63 → 64 → 65 → 66 (reasoning models, RL with verifiable rewards, diffusion LMs, evaluator-driven discovery agents).
