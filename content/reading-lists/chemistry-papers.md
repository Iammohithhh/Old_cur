title: "Machine Learning for Chemistry: reading list"

_Curated 2026-11. ⭐ = essential (read first). Links: DOI for journal papers, arXiv abs page otherwise._

Scope: molecular ML (representations, property prediction, generative design, reactions/retrosynthesis, ML for quantum chemistry, LLMs/agents, Bayesian optimization for experimentation). Interatomic potentials, materials/crystals, and proteins/biology are covered in other lists.

## 1. Orientation: field-defining reviews
1. ⭐ [Machine learning for molecular and materials science](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-018-0337-2) — Butler et al., *Nature* 2018. The canonical map of the field: data sources, representations, model classes, and the open problems that set the agenda for the following years.
2. [Inverse molecular design using machine learning: Generative models for matter engineering](https://doi.org/10.1126/science.aat2663) — Sanchez-Lengeling & Aspuru-Guzik, *Science* 2018. Frames molecular design as inverting a property map and organizes the early generative approaches (VAEs, RL, GANs) around that framing.
3. [Machine learning-aided generative molecular design](https://doi.org/10.1038/s42256-024-00843-5) — Du et al., *Nature Machine Intelligence* 2024. Modern review that treats generation and filtering as one pipeline, covering 2D/3D generators, conditioning, and evaluation gaps.

## 2. Molecular representations & property prediction
4. [SMILES, a chemical language and information system. 1. Introduction to methodology and encoding rules](https://doi.org/10.1021/ci00057a005) — Weininger, *J. Chem. Inf. Comput. Sci.* 1988. The string representation that later made sequence models (RNNs, Transformers) usable for chemistry; you need it to understand every "chemical language model".
5. ⭐ [Extended-connectivity fingerprints](https://doi.org/10.1021/ci100050t) — Rogers & Hahn, *J. Chem. Inf. Model.* 2010. Defines ECFP/Morgan fingerprints, a hashed neighbourhood-aggregation scheme that is the baseline every learned representation must beat.
6. [Self-referencing embedded strings (SELFIES): A 100% robust molecular string representation](https://doi.org/10.1088/2632-2153/aba947) — Krenn et al., *Mach. Learn.: Sci. Technol.* 2020. A grammar in which every string decodes to a valid molecular graph, removing the invalid-output problem for string-based generators.
7. [Convolutional networks on graphs for learning molecular fingerprints](https://arxiv.org/abs/1509.09292) — Duvenaud et al., *NeurIPS* 2015. Makes ECFP differentiable: shows that a fingerprint can be learned end-to-end as a graph neural network.
8. ⭐ [Neural message passing for quantum chemistry](https://arxiv.org/abs/1704.01212) — Gilmer et al., *ICML* 2017. Unifies graph neural networks under the message-passing (MPNN) framework and sets the template for property prediction on QM9.
9. ⭐ [Analyzing learned molecular representations for property prediction](https://doi.org/10.1021/acs.jcim.9b00237) — Yang et al., *J. Chem. Inf. Model.* 2019. Introduces the directed (bond-centred) MPNN behind Chemprop and a careful comparison against fingerprints using scaffold splits; a model of rigorous evaluation.
10. [Strategies for pre-training graph neural networks](https://arxiv.org/abs/1905.12265) — Hu et al., *ICLR* 2020. Systematic study of node-level and graph-level self-supervised pretraining for molecules, including when pretraining causes negative transfer.
11. [Large-scale chemical language representations capture molecular structure and properties](https://doi.org/10.1038/s42256-022-00580-7) — Ross et al., *Nature Machine Intelligence* 2022. MoLFormer: a linear-attention Transformer pretrained on ~1.1B SMILES, showing what scale buys a chemical language model.
12. [Uni-Mol: A universal 3D molecular representation learning framework](https://doi.org/10.26434/chemrxiv-2022-jjm0j) — Zhou et al., *ICLR* 2023 (ChemRxiv DOI). SE(3)-aware Transformer pretrained on hundreds of millions of conformations; the reference point for 3D-structure-based molecular pretraining.
13. [Chemprop: A machine learning package for chemical property prediction](https://doi.org/10.1021/acs.jcim.3c01250) — Heid et al., *J. Chem. Inf. Model.* 2024. Software paper for the de facto standard D-MPNN toolkit; covers multitask, reaction, and uncertainty extensions you will use as baselines.

## 3. Benchmarks, baselines & critiques for property prediction
14. [Quantum chemistry structures and properties of 134 kilo molecules](https://doi.org/10.1038/sdata.2014.22) — Ramakrishnan et al., *Scientific Data* 2014. The QM9 dataset; know its construction and limits (small, equilibrium-only molecules) before trusting results reported on it.
15. ⭐ [MoleculeNet: a benchmark for molecular machine learning](https://doi.org/10.1039/C7SC02664A) — Wu et al., *Chemical Science* 2018. The standard benchmark suite, with metrics and random/scaffold splitting protocols; also the source of many later criticisms of benchmark practice.
16. [Exposing the limitations of molecular machine learning with activity cliffs](https://doi.org/10.1021/acs.jcim.2c01073) — van Tilborg et al., *J. Chem. Inf. Model.* 2022. Negative result: deep models underperform descriptor-based baselines on activity cliffs (small structural change, large property change). Introduces the MoleculeACE benchmark.
17. [A systematic study of key elements underlying molecular property prediction](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-023-41948-6) — Deng et al., *Nature Communications* 2023. Trains tens of thousands of models to show that representation-learning models often fail to beat fixed fingerprints, and that dataset size and splits drive conclusions.
18. [Practically significant method comparison protocols for machine learning in small molecule drug discovery](https://doi.org/10.1021/acs.jcim.5c01609) — Ash et al., *J. Chem. Inf. Model.* 2025. Community guidelines on statistics, repeated cross-validation, and practical significance for comparing molecular ML methods.

## 4. Molecular generative models
19. ⭐ [Automatic chemical design using a data-driven continuous representation of molecules](https://doi.org/10.1021/acscentsci.7b00572) — Gómez-Bombarelli et al., *ACS Central Science* 2018. The SMILES VAE: learns a continuous latent space where gradient-based optimization of properties becomes possible; the starting point for latent-space design.
20. [Molecular de-novo design through deep reinforcement learning](https://doi.org/10.1186/s13321-017-0235-x) — Olivecrona et al., *J. Cheminformatics* 2017. REINVENT: fine-tunes a SMILES RNN prior with policy-gradient RL plus a prior-likelihood penalty; still one of the strongest goal-directed baselines.
21. [Junction tree variational autoencoder for molecular graph generation](https://arxiv.org/abs/1802.04364) — Jin et al., *ICML* 2018. Generates graphs coarse-to-fine through a tree of substructures, guaranteeing valid molecules; the key graph-VAE design.
22. [Flow network based generative models for non-iterative diverse candidate generation](https://arxiv.org/abs/2106.04399) — Bengio et al., *NeurIPS* 2021. Introduces GFlowNets, which sample objects in proportion to a reward and so return diverse high-scoring molecules rather than a single optimum.
23. [DiGress: Discrete denoising diffusion for graph generation](https://arxiv.org/abs/2209.14734) — Vignac et al., *ICLR* 2023. Discrete diffusion over node and edge categories with a marginal-preserving noise model; the reference 2D graph diffusion model.
24. ⭐ [Equivariant diffusion for molecule generation in 3D](https://arxiv.org/abs/2203.17003) — Hoogeboom et al., *ICML* 2022. EDM: an E(3)-equivariant diffusion model jointly over coordinates and atom types; it launched 3D molecular diffusion.
25. [Geometric latent diffusion models for 3D molecule generation](https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.01140) — Xu et al., *ICML* 2023. GeoLDM: runs diffusion in an equivariant latent space from an autoencoder, which improves validity and controllability over EDM.
26. [SemlaFlow – Efficient 3D molecular generation with latent attention and equivariant flow matching](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.07266) — Irwin et al., *AISTATS* 2025. Equivariant flow matching with scale optimal transport and a latent-attention architecture; matches diffusion quality in ~20–100 steps, about 100x faster sampling.
27. [All-atom diffusion transformers: Unified generative modelling of molecules and materials](https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.03965) — Joshi et al., *ICML* 2025. ADiT: a non-equivariant Transformer latent diffusion model over a shared all-atom latent space, trained jointly on molecules and crystals, with predictable gains from scaling.
28. [Generative artificial intelligence for navigating synthesizable chemical space](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.03494) — Gao, Luo & Coley, arXiv 2024 (journal version reported in *PNAS* 2025). SynFormer generates synthetic pathways (building blocks plus reaction templates) instead of molecules, so synthesizability is built in rather than filtered afterwards.

## 5. Evaluating generative models
29. [GuacaMol: Benchmarking models for de novo molecular design](https://doi.org/10.1021/acs.jcim.8b00839) — Brown et al., *J. Chem. Inf. Model.* 2019. Standard distribution-learning and goal-directed benchmark tasks; shows that simple genetic-algorithm and SMILES-LSTM baselines are hard to beat.
30. [Molecular sets (MOSES): A benchmarking platform for molecular generation models](https://doi.org/10.3389/fphar.2020.565644) — Polykovskiy et al., *Frontiers in Pharmacology* 2020. Standardized dataset, splits, and distribution metrics (validity, uniqueness, novelty, FCD, scaffold similarity) for unconditional generation.
31. ⭐ [On failure modes in molecule generation and optimization](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ddtec.2020.09.003) — Renz et al., *Drug Discovery Today: Technologies* 2019. Critical paper: a trivial model can score highly on distribution metrics, and goal-directed optimizers exploit scoring functions. Essential for designing honest evaluations.
32. [The synthesizability of molecules proposed by generative models](https://doi.org/10.1021/acs.jcim.0c00174) — Gao & Coley, *J. Chem. Inf. Model.* 2020. Uses retrosynthesis planning to show that many "optimal" generated molecules are impractical to make, and that heuristic synthesizability scores are only partial proxies.
33. [Sample efficiency matters: A benchmark for practical molecular optimization](https://arxiv.org/abs/2206.12411) — Gao et al., *NeurIPS* 2022 (Datasets & Benchmarks). The PMO benchmark: compares 25 optimizers under a fixed oracle-call budget and finds that older methods such as REINVENT remain competitive.

## 6. Reaction prediction & retrosynthesis
34. ⭐ [Planning chemical syntheses with deep neural networks and symbolic AI](https://doi.org/10.1038/nature25978) — Segler, Preuss & Waller, *Nature* 2018. 3N-MCTS: neural policy networks over reaction templates guide Monte Carlo tree search, and chemists preferred its routes in blind tests. The AlphaGo moment for synthesis planning.
35. ⭐ [Molecular Transformer: A model for uncertainty-calibrated chemical reaction prediction](https://doi.org/10.1021/acscentsci.9b00576) — Schwaller et al., *ACS Central Science* 2019. Frames reaction prediction as SMILES-to-SMILES machine translation with a template-free Transformer, with calibrated confidence scores.
36. [Deep retrosynthetic reaction prediction using local reactivity and global attention](https://doi.org/10.1021/jacsau.1c00246) — Chen & Jung, *JACS Au* 2021. LocalRetro: scores local (atom/bond-centred) templates with a GNN plus global attention; a strong, interpretable single-step baseline.
37. [Predicting reaction performance in C–N cross-coupling using machine learning](https://doi.org/10.1126/science.aar5169) — Ahneman et al., *Science* 2018. Random forests on computed descriptors predict yields across a high-throughput dataset; the flagship yield-prediction study (read it together with the next paper).
38. [Comment on "Predicting reaction performance in C–N cross-coupling using machine learning"](https://doi.org/10.1126/science.aat8603) — Chuang & Keiser, *Science* 2018. Critical result: random-feature baselines perform comparably, so good test scores do not show the model learned chemistry. A lesson in adversarial controls.
39. [Machine learning for chemical reactivity: The importance of failed experiments](https://doi.org/10.1002/anie.202204647) — Strieth-Kalthoff et al., *Angewandte Chemie Int. Ed.* 2022. Shows how reporting bias and missing negative data in literature corpora distort reactivity models; argues for publishing failed experiments.
40. [Re-evaluating retrosynthesis algorithms with Syntheseus](https://doi.org/10.1039/D4FD00093E) — Maziarz et al., *Faraday Discussions* 2025. A benchmarking library that fixes common evaluation pitfalls in single- and multi-step retrosynthesis; rankings of state-of-the-art models change under controlled evaluation.
41. ⭐ [Electron flow matching for generative reaction mechanism prediction](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-025-09426-9) — Joung et al., *Nature* 2025. FlowER: flow matching over bond-electron matrices that conserves mass and electrons by construction, predicting mechanistic steps instead of only products.
42. [Chemist-aligned retrosynthesis by ensembling diverse inductive bias models](https://arxiv.org/abs/2412.05269) — Maziarz et al., *Nature* 2026 (arXiv preprint linked). RetroChimera: learning-to-rank ensemble of a graph-edit model and a Transformer generator; chemists preferred its predictions in blinded comparisons, and it generalizes out of distribution.

## 7. ML for quantum chemistry
43. [Fast and accurate modeling of molecular atomization energies with machine learning](https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevLett.108.058301) — Rupp et al., *Physical Review Letters* 2012. Kernel ridge regression on the Coulomb matrix; the paper that started learning quantum-chemical properties directly from structure.
44. [Big data meets quantum chemistry approximations: The Δ-machine learning approach](https://doi.org/10.1021/acs.jctc.5b00099) — Ramakrishnan et al., *J. Chem. Theory Comput.* 2015. Learn the correction from a cheap method to an expensive one rather than the property itself; a widely reused data-efficiency trick.
45. ⭐ [Pushing the frontiers of density functionals by solving the fractional electron problem](https://doi.org/10.1126/science.abj6511) — Kirkpatrick et al., *Science* 2021. DM21: a neural exchange-correlation functional trained with fractional-charge and fractional-spin constraints, showing that physical constraints can be learned from data.
46. [Accurate and scalable exchange-correlation with deep learning](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.14665) — Luise et al., arXiv 2025. Skala: a neural XC functional that learns non-local features on the integration grid from large, high-accuracy wavefunction datasets, reaching hybrid-level accuracy at semi-local cost.
47. ⭐ [Ab initio solution of the many-electron Schrödinger equation with deep neural networks](https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevResearch.2.033429) — Pfau et al., *Physical Review Research* 2020. FermiNet: a permutation-equivariant network as a variational Monte Carlo ansatz, reaching near-exact energies without a basis set.
48. [Deep-neural-network solution of the electronic Schrödinger equation](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41557-020-0544-y) — Hermann, Schätzle & Noé, *Nature Chemistry* 2020. PauliNet: builds physics (Hartree–Fock orbitals, cusps, Jastrow factors) into the neural ansatz for efficiency.
49. [A self-attention ansatz for ab-initio quantum chemistry](https://arxiv.org/abs/2211.13672) — von Glehn, Spencer & Pfau, *ICLR* 2023. Psiformer: replaces FermiNet's architecture with self-attention, improving accuracy especially for larger systems.
50. [Ab initio quantum chemistry with neural-network wavefunctions](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41570-023-00516-8) — Hermann et al., *Nature Reviews Chemistry* 2023. Review of neural VMC: ansatz design, optimization, excited states, and remaining scaling challenges.
51. [An ab initio foundation model of wavefunctions that accurately describes chemical bond breaking](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.19960) — Foster et al., arXiv 2025. Orbformer: a wavefunction model pretrained across ~22k structures and fine-tuned per system, amortizing neural VMC cost and handling multireference bond dissociation.
52. [Unifying machine learning and quantum chemistry with a deep neural network for molecular wavefunctions](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-019-12875-2) — Schütt et al., *Nature Communications* 2019. SchNOrb: predicts Hamiltonian and overlap matrices in an atomic-orbital basis, giving orbitals and derived properties from one model.
53. [SE(3)-equivariant prediction of molecular wavefunctions and electronic densities](https://arxiv.org/abs/2106.02347) — Unke et al., *NeurIPS* 2021. PhiSNet: an equivariant architecture that respects the rotational transformation of Hamiltonian matrix blocks.
54. [Efficient and equivariant graph networks for predicting quantum Hamiltonian](https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.04922) — Yu et al., *ICML* 2023. QHNet: reduces the tensor-product cost of equivariant Hamiltonian prediction, making ML Hamiltonians cheaper to train and use.
55. [Machine learning for electronically excited states of molecules](https://doi.org/10.1021/acs.chemrev.0c00749) — Westermayr & Marquetand, *Chemical Reviews* 2021. Review of ML for excited-state energies, couplings, and spectra, and the specific difficulties (phase arbitrariness, state crossings).
56. [Leveraging infrared spectroscopy for automated structure elucidation](https://doi.org/10.1038/s42004-024-01341-w) — Alberts et al., *Communications Chemistry* 2024. Spectrum-to-structure as sequence generation: a Transformer pretrained on simulated IR spectra and fine-tuned on experimental ones, a template for ML inverse spectroscopy.

## 8. LLMs & agents for chemistry
57. [Leveraging large language models for predictive chemistry](https://doi.org/10.1038/s42256-023-00788-1) — Jablonka et al., *Nature Machine Intelligence* 2024. Fine-tuned GPT-3 on text-formatted questions matches or beats specialist models in the low-data regime; establishes LLMs as general-purpose regressors and classifiers.
58. [Augmenting large language models with chemistry tools](https://doi.org/10.1038/s42256-024-00832-8) — Bran et al., *Nature Machine Intelligence* 2024. ChemCrow: a ReAct-style agent that orchestrates expert-designed tools. Notable for showing that LLM judges fail to tell correct from incorrect agent outputs, which matters for evaluation.
59. ⭐ [Autonomous chemical research with large language models](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-023-06792-0) — Boiko et al., *Nature* 2023. Coscientist: a planner LLM with web search, documentation retrieval, code execution, and instrument-API modules. The reference architecture for LLM-driven experimentation agents.
60. ⭐ [A framework for evaluating the chemical knowledge and reasoning abilities of large language models against the expertise of chemists](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41557-025-01815-x) — Mirza et al., *Nature Chemistry* 2025. ChemBench: 2,700+ curated questions with human-expert baselines; top LLMs beat average experts yet fail basic tasks and are poorly calibrated.
61. [Assessing the chemical intelligence of large language models](https://doi.org/10.1021/acs.jcim.5c02145) — Runcie, Deane & Imrie, *J. Chem. Inf. Model.* 2026. ChemIQ: short-answer (not multiple-choice) questions on molecular comprehension and reasoning; reasoning models score far above non-reasoning ones, and the reasoning traces are analysed.
62. [Training a scientific reasoning model for chemistry](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.17238) — Narayanan et al., *NeurIPS* 2025. ether0: a 24B model trained with reinforcement learning from verifiable rewards plus distillation over hundreds of molecular tasks; shows that RL reasoning recipes transfer to structure-output chemistry tasks.
63. [Chemical reasoning in LLMs unlocks strategy-aware synthesis planning and reaction mechanism elucidation](https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.08537) — Bran et al., arXiv 2025. Uses a general LLM as a natural-language-steerable evaluator over search, combining classical route search with LLM judgement of strategy.
64. [Collective intelligence for AI-assisted chemical synthesis](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-026-10131-4) — Li et al., *Nature* 2026. MOSAIC: partitions reaction space into thousands of specialist fine-tuned 8B LLM experts with routing by embedding distance, giving an interpretable confidence metric.
65. [A review of large language models and autonomous agents in chemistry](https://doi.org/10.1039/D4SC03921A) — Ramos, Collison & White, *Chemical Science* 2025. Comprehensive survey of chemistry LLMs, tool-using agents, benchmarks, and open problems (evaluation, data quality, safety).

## 9. Bayesian optimization & self-driving-lab AI design
66. [Phoenics: A Bayesian optimizer for chemistry](https://doi.org/10.1021/acscentsci.8b00307) — Häse et al., *ACS Central Science* 2018. Bayesian-kernel-density surrogate with parallelizable, tunable exploration designed for experiment planning.
67. [Gryffin: An algorithm for Bayesian optimization of categorical variables informed by expert knowledge](https://doi.org/10.1063/5.0048164) — Häse et al., *Applied Physics Reviews* 2021. Extends Phoenics to categorical choices and uses physicochemical descriptors to inject domain knowledge into the search.
68. [ChemOS: Orchestrating autonomous experimentation](https://doi.org/10.1126/scirobotics.aat5559) — Roch et al., *Science Robotics* 2018. A software architecture for closed-loop labs that decouples planners, instruments, and databases; the blueprint for SDL orchestration.
69. ⭐ [Bayesian reaction optimization as a tool for chemical synthesis](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-021-03213-y) — Shields et al., *Nature* 2021. EDBO: GP-based BO with chemical descriptors, benchmarked head-to-head against human experts; the clearest evidence that BO beats human intuition at experiment selection.
70. [A sober look at LLMs for material discovery: Are they actually good for Bayesian optimization over molecules?](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.05015) — Kristiadi et al., *ICML* 2024. Critical study: LLM features help molecular BO only after domain-specific pretraining or fine-tuning (e.g., with Laplace-approximated PEFT surrogates), and general-purpose LLMs do not.
71. [Self-driving laboratories for chemistry and materials science](https://doi.org/10.1021/acs.chemrev.4c00055) — Tom et al., *Chemical Reviews* 2024. Survey of SDL decision-making algorithms (BO variants, multi-objective, multi-fidelity), software stacks, and benchmarks.

## Learning path
Suggested reading order (by numbers) in 3 stages: Foundations → Core methods → Frontier (2024–26).

**Stage 1 — Foundations (orientation, representations, evaluation literacy).**
1 → 4 → 5 → 7 → 8 → 9 → 14 → 15 → 16 → 43 → 44 → 19 → 31 → 34 → 35.
Goal: know the classic representations and baselines, how benchmarks are built, and why fingerprints remain strong. Read the landmark generative, retrosynthesis, and quantum-chemistry ML papers alongside one critique each.

**Stage 2 — Core methods (one deep pass per sub-area).**
- Property prediction and pretraining: 6 → 10 → 11 → 12 → 13 → 17.
- Generation: 2 → 20 → 21 → 22 → 23 → 24 → 25, then evaluation 29 → 30 → 32 → 33.
- Reactions: 36 → 37 → 38 → 39.
- Quantum chemistry: 45 → 47 → 48 → 49 → 50 → 52 → 53 → 54 → 55.
- LLMs and experimentation: 57 → 58 → 59 → 66 → 67 → 68 → 69.

**Stage 3 — Frontier (2024–26).**
3 → 18 → 26 → 27 → 28 → 40 → 41 → 42 → 46 → 51 → 56 → 60 → 61 → 62 → 63 → 64 → 65 → 70 → 71.
Themes: flow-matching and unified all-atom generators; synthesis-aware design; mechanism-level, conservation-respecting reaction models; neural XC functionals and wavefunction foundation models; RL-trained chemistry reasoning LLMs; and rigorous evaluation (Syntheseus, ChemBench/ChemIQ, method-comparison protocols, the sober BO study).
